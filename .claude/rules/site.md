# site/

## Conventions

- The design system is the Design System artifact https://claude.ai/artifact/D3TtwiZtWyM175ydwyWCyv — read its README before styling anything. `app/globals.css` is its code form.
- `site/` is an Elysia 2 app (`elysia` pinned to `2.0.0-rc.0` — a release candidate, so a bump is a deliberate step, and Dependabot ignores it) that renders KitaJS JSX (`@kitajs/html`, the `jsxImportSource` in `tsconfig.json`) to strings: there is no React and no client framework. `app/site.tsx` holds the routes and the 404 and 500 handlers (`onError('global', …)`, the first calls of the chain, since a hook reaches only the routes added after it — placed last they answered a path no route matched and left a route's own error to Elysia's problem+json — so they answer both), a marketplace's routes and a share link's card and credits are `group`s whose `derive` looks the marketplace or the code up once and answers a miss itself with `status(404, …)` (a page group answers with the HTML page, a card group with a line of text; `status()` rather than a thrown error class, since `onError` handlers run in registration order and the catch-all, which must come first to reach every route, would answer before a class-specific one), a route that wants `FIXED`'s `cache-control` says `{ fixed: true }` (the `fixed` macro, which leaves a header the response already carries, so a 404 keeps its own), and `/builder/:code` takes no schema, since `readShared` reads a bad `?i=` as the first palette on purpose, and every response goes through `page()` (`app/document.tsx`: the document, its status, its `cache-control` and an `etag` hashed from its bytes, which Vercel's CDN answers a browser's revalidation with as 304, a cache miss included) — a route passes its `path`, and a page with one carries `<link rel="canonical">` and `og:url` on the production origin (`SHARE_URL`'s, `src/core/own.ts`), so a preview deployment or the git-branch alias never stands in for it, a route passes its `ld` graphs, which `Document` writes as JSON-LD (`lib/seo.ts`: the landing's `WebSite` and `SoftwareSourceCode` — not `SoftwareApplication`, whose rich result wants ratings or reviews ttheme does not collect, so Search Console would list the landing as an invalid item, and `crumbs` for a marketplace's page and a palette's), and its `robots` — `max-image-preview:large` unless the route says otherwise, and `noindex` for a share link's page, which never gets a `Disallow: /builder/` in `robots.txt` because a link preview's crawler obeys that too and would stop reading the card the link exists for; every page's title and description come from `lib/titles.ts`, so each prerendered page has its own (`paletteMeta`'s title is also what `client/marketplace-workspace.tsx` sets as `document.title` when a palette changes in place); every page carries `og:type`, `og:site_name`, `og:locale` and a `twitter:card` (`summary`, or `summary_large_image` with a card, whose `og:image:type`, `og:image:alt` and `twitter:image:alt` name the palette) and links `/apple-touch-icon.png`; a route passes its card as a path, which `Document` puts on the production origin too. `og:image` carries `?v=` and `CARD_REV` (`lib/seo.ts`: the version and an `embed.ts` macro's hash of the renderer's sources), since Discord, Slack and X keep an image by its URL for days and a card redrawn at the same address kept showing the old one. Every page a visitor shares has a card (`lib/card.ts`, below): a palette page its own at its path plus `/card.png`, a marketplace's page its own at its path plus `/card.png`, a share link its code's, and the landing and `/marketplace` `/card.png`, the first lead's (`fallback`); the 404 and 500 pages have none. `/apple-touch-icon.png` (`touchIconPng` in `lib/tab-icon.ts`, 180×180) is the `>ω` mark (below) at 0.85 of the icon on the first lead's background, opaque and square, since iOS rounds a home-screen icon itself and takes none from an SVG `rel=icon`; `/favicon.ico` (`faviconIco`: the mark at 32 and 48 and its hand-placed 16×16 pixels, `PIXELS`, as three PNGs in one ICO container, each opaque) is for a crawler that does not read the page's data-URI `rel=icon`, and `/robots.txt` allows every path and names `/sitemap.xml` (`sitemapXml`: a `loc` for every `PRERENDERED` path and no `lastmod`, since a page rebuilt on every deploy has no date worth saying); the cards and the icons come from the function, cached at the CDN like the pages. A card is one SVG string that `raster()` (`lib/raster.ts`, `@resvg/resvg-wasm`) turns into a PNG: the page's terminal window, wearing the palette, on its own facet ground (`facets` over `lib/facets.ts`' `shade` at half strength, mixed in OKLab by `src/core/oklab.ts`), the `ttheme` lockup at the top left (`wordmark`: the mark before the wordmark's path, `wordmarkSvg`, as the header draws it), the name in the cursor color at the left — two rows, split at a `-` or an `@`, when one row would come out under 60px —, its work (the publisher for an original, `shared palette` for a share link), and under it, for a palette of a work that is not built-in, its publisher, the three signature dots, and in the window the shell scene and the sixteen ANSI colors; a marketplace's card lists its works' leads first in the window (`leadsOf`), and the site card's `HEADLINE` — `use your favorite` over `anime character`, `in your terminal` under it — is the one wording the user chose, its verb the command's (`ttheme use`). `raster()` embeds its own wasm and fonts: the macros in `lib/embed.ts` run when a module is transpiled or bundled and inline the resvg wasm, Nunito 900, Inter 500 and JetBrains Mono 400 and 700 (subset to sfnt with `subset-font`, since resvg reads no woff2) and a BIZ UDGothic piece for the `☆` of `Lucky☆Star`, which resvg finds by falling back glyph by glyph through the fonts it holds, so the bundle reads no file and runs from a folder with no `node_modules`; a font's family is read from its `name` table and never typed, since a Fontsource static Nunito calls itself `Nunito ExtraLight Black` and a pinned Inter instance keeps the family and weight of its variable font, and a text is measured by rendering it (`ink`, `fit`: resvg's `getBBox`), which also aligns a big name by its ink and not by its advance. A character the fonts lack draws as nothing. Measured 2026-10-12 on the bundle alone: about 110 ms a card, a first render about 200 ms, the process holding at about 300 MB through 298 cards and 24 at once, and `server.js` growing from 1.25 to 2.72 MB. `lib/tab-icon.ts` keeps the two icons on the pure-TypeScript `encodeRgb`, and `site/lib/tab-icon.test.ts` holds that a rectangle with no corner is painted whole, which it once was not (a half-bright background on every card and on the touch icon). `@elysia/html` is not used because its `mapResponse` is plugin-scoped and never reaches the outer app's 404 and 500. `server.ts` is Vercel's entry (`export default`; Vercel refuses `listen`), `dev.ts` the local server; both `use(autoHead())` (`elysia/auto-head`) first, since Elysia 2 answers `HEAD` only through it, and without it every page and the `og:image` answered a link preview's or a monitor's `HEAD` with 404.
- The site works in every browser Baseline calls widely available — on 2026-10-22 Chrome and Edge 124, Firefox 125 and Safari 17.4 on macOS and iOS, with Android WebView 124 and Samsung Internet 27 (`baseline-browser-mapping`) — and in those every page reads, every link and copy button works and every widget runs; what came later is an enhancement whose fallback is named where it is described (anchor positioning, scroll-driven animations, `@starting-style`, `::details-content` and `interpolate-size`, view transitions within and across documents, the Navigation API and `pagereveal`, Speculation Rules, `scroll-initial-target`, `text-wrap: pretty`, `field-sizing`), relative colors sit behind `@supports` over a `color-mix()` fallback, a newer DOM API behind a check (`'navigation' in window`, `'startViewTransition' in document`), and `client/tsconfig.json` holds the browser code to that floor's built-ins (below). The one exception is `light-dark()` (Safari 17.5; Baseline widely available on 2026-11-13): the slots are registered `<color>`s, which take no `light-dark()` there, so in Safari 17.4 a palette that is not the page's — a preview window, a tile, a swatch — shows in the page's colors, and one drawn through an unregistered var (`--sg0`) not at all. Playwright's WebKit builds run a newer WebKit than the Safari they are named for (its 17.4 has `light-dark()` and relative colors), so a CSS feature's floor is read from `web-features`, not from them. Checked 2026-10-22 on `/`, `/sheets`, `/marketplace`, a palette's workspace, `/builder` and a share link at 1280 and 390px in Playwright's WebKit 17.4 and Chromium 125 builds, WebKit 27.2, Chrome 155 and Firefox 158: every custom element defined and no script error, once the builder stopped throwing on an iterator helper in WebKit 17.4; Firefox 158 lacks scroll-driven animations and `interpolate-size`, which fall back as described.
- UI parts live in `components/ui/*`: KitaJS components whose looks are `cva` variants in their own file, never a long `class` passed from a feature component. Every part renders `data-slot` (and `data-variant`/`data-size`) and carries no `dark:` variants — tokens do the theming. Behavior comes from native elements, not a library: `Dialog` is a `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()` (focus trap, Esc, `::backdrop`; `:root:has(dialog:modal)` locks the page's scroll), a `ToggleGroup` is a radio group (`has-checked:` styles, arrow keys for free; its `underline` variant draws tabs over a hairline as wide as their row, the checked one underlined in `primary`, and `ToggleGroupCount` puts a count pill in a tab — `/marketplace`'s source tabs), `TabsList` a `role="tablist"` whose keys `client/tabs.ts` moves. A toggle that only picks which panel shows needs no script: the marketplace workspace's install step and scene are radio groups whose section is a named `group`, and each panel is `hidden` with a `group-has-[input[value=…]:checked]/<group>:` variant, spelled out per value since Tailwind only generates classes it finds whole in the source. Icons are `lucide` nodes drawn by `Icon` (`lib/icons.tsx`), and a brand wears its own mark: `simple-icons` (CC0, each mark taken from the brand's published logo) drawn by `BrandIcon`, filled with `currentColor` and set before the brand's name — GitHub on the header's and footer's links and every repository line, npm in the footer, and pixiv, X, Bluesky and Patreon on an artist's links (`PictureLink.kind`, which `lib/credits.ts` fills from `linksOf` and `pageKind`); running text keeps the word, and a brand it does not carry stays a word. A link off the site opens in a new tab (`target="_blank" rel="noopener"`, without `noreferrer`, so GitHub and npm still see where a visit came from) and ends in `NewTab` — but the header's star button, drawn as GitHub's own, and a marketplace card's repository and `updated` buttons, which say so in words only — (`lib/icons.tsx`): Lucide's `ArrowUpRight`, sized like the brand mark beside it, and `, opens in a new tab` for a screen reader — in the footer inside the label's `span` (`OutLabel`), since `[data-glide]` anchors its underline on every `span` a link holds directly, and a second one would pull the underline onto the hidden text, with the label's last word and the arrow in one `whitespace-nowrap` span, since a line may break before any inline SVG and a label that wraps in the narrow columns would leave the arrow alone on a line. `simple-icons` is one 5.3 MB module: only the marks a page imports reach a bundle (`sideEffects: false`), but an inline source map would carry all of it, so the dev build links its map (`main.js.map`) instead of inlining it.
- Interactivity is vanilla TypeScript in `client/`, bundled by `Bun.build` into one module: each page's widget is a custom element (`lead-showcase`, `tab-showcase`, `facet-ground`, `route-progress`, `marketplace-board`, `marketplace-workspace`, `palette-picker`, `sparkle-burst`, `kaomoji-rain`; declared for JSX in `custom-elements.d.ts`) — but for `palette-builder` (`client/palette-builder.tsx`), the builder's, which `client()` (`build.ts`) builds as a second entry point beside `main.ts`, without splitting, and `assets.json` names as `builder`, so only `/builder` loads it (`page()`'s `module`) and every other page's `main.js` stays as it was (the builder's 311 KB, 85 KB gzip, carry the gate, the mirror and its HCT library, the palette text and code reader and `smol-toml`; measured 2026-10-22: `main.js` grew 0.3 KB when the builder came) that reads its data from a `<script type="application/json">` the server wrote with `json()` (`lib/render.ts`, which escapes `<`, `>` and `&`) — but a list of palettes, which `marketplace-workspace` fetches once the page has painted (`afterPaint`: a `requestAnimationFrame` and a task after it, so the list never competes with what draws the first screen) or at the first move that needs it (`palettes()` in `client/palettes.ts`: one request per file, which a page shares with the palette picker, at `priority: 'low'`, forgotten when it fails so the next use asks again) from `palettesPath` (`lib/gallery.ts`: `/marketplace/<id>/palettes.json`, the built-in marketplace's `ttheme` among them), files `build.ts` writes beside the pages (`DATA`, `app/site.tsx`) so the CDN answers them as it answers a page; inline, the built-in palettes were half of `/sheets`' and of every built-in palette page's HTML (54 KB brotli'd, 27 KB of it the list, measured 2026-10-22), every one of 151 pages carried the same list, and the simulated LCP of `/sheets` and a palette's page on Lighthouse's slow phone came 150 ms sooner without it. A widget never waits on that list to show what the page drew: until it arrives a palette link is followed as a link, a step, an arrow, a tab or a tile waits for it and then acts (`#when`) — and re-renders a changed fragment with the same KitaJS component the server used (`html()`, then `fill`/`swap` from `client/dom.ts`), so every piece of markup has one implementation. A copy button is any `[data-copy]` button, handled by one delegated listener (`client/copy.ts`): it keeps its label, says `copied` through the page's one polite live region (`[data-announce]`, a `role="status"` that `Document` renders empty at the end of `<body>`, so a change in it is announced, written by `announce()` in `client/dom.ts`) — a filter that narrows a list says how many palettes it left there too, through `tally()` half a second after the last keystroke (the palette picker, `/marketplace`'s search and source tabs, the workspace's sidebar search), and a previous or next button names where it goes (`previous palette, <name>` in the workspace) while the visible note (`copied (・ω・)`) is `aria-hidden`, and when the clipboard refuses — no secure context, an in-app browser without the permission, a denied one — it selects the command's text (or the button's) and says to press ⌘c (on Apple's platforms, `data-apple`) or ctrl+c, three times as long. A command's `<code>` selects as a whole (`select-all`), so a double-click takes all of `npx @kecan0406/ttheme init` rather than one word of it.
- Every page has one `<main id="content">` — the landing's and the 404's content wrapper, `MarketplaceFrame`'s, the marketplace workspace's right pane and the builder's stage — and `Document` puts a `skip to content` link to it first in `<body>`: a `primary` pill fixed at the top left, above the header, that stays `sr-only` until it has focus (`not-focus:sr-only`), so the first Tab on any page offers it and Enter sends the next Tab into the page past the header. `html`'s `scroll-padding-top` (64px: the 52px the stuck header leaves on screen and a gap) keeps whatever the browser scrolls into view — a focused element, the link's or an anchor's target — clear of the header (WCAG 2.4.11): measured 2026-10-11, Shift+Tab back through the landing's character tiles left each row at the window's top edge, under the header's glass, and now leaves it 64px down. The header's own links, buttons and radios move their scroll target down by the same `--clearance` (`scroll-margin-block`, outside an open popover, whose list keeps its own `scroll-mt-8`), since they sit inside that 64px themselves: Chrome counted them as hidden and scrolled each one it focused to the middle of the window, a click on the light or dark switch (a `<label>` focuses its radio) or a Tab through the header moving the page 456px up at 1280×900 while the sticky header stayed put — measured 2026-10-21 on every page with the header at 1280 and 390px, the page now stays where it was; WebKit focuses nothing on a click (measured in Playwright's WebKit 26.6), so it never moved there.
- In forced colors (Windows' contrast themes) the browser drops every `box-shadow` and paints every author background `Canvas`, keeping only system colors, so the `ring` focus styles, the glide underline, a checked `ToggleGroup` item's or character tile's ring and the landing's progress line all vanished — measured 2026-10-11 under Chrome's forced-colors emulation: no `Button`, wordmark or picker trigger showed keyboard focus. `globals.css` draws it there as a 2px `Highlight` outline, on the focused element or on the label around a visually hidden radio (`:has(> .sr-only:focus-visible)`), underlines a checked one's label in `Highlight` and paints the underline and the progress line `Highlight` (`!important` against the footer's `--key` rules); a system color survives forced colors with no `forced-color-adjust`. A swatch — the signature dots (`Signature`, the picker trigger's), `Swatch`, the landing's and the footer's ANSI pills, and the one-element chips (`signature-dots`, `work-tile`, `work-swatch`, `palette-dots`) — takes `forced-color-adjust: none`, since its color is the content and forced colors painted it `Canvas`; nothing that holds text does. `prefers-contrast: more` lifts the muted, soft and faint foregrounds and the borders toward the foreground and makes the glass nearly opaque, and `prefers-reduced-transparency` makes it opaque.
- A page's keyboard shortcuts go through `client/keys.ts`. A single key never fires with ⌘, ctrl or alt held (Alt+← and ⌘+← are the browser's Back), for a key the page already handled (`defaultPrevented`), in a field (`typing`: input, textarea, select, contenteditable) or during an IME composition (`composing`): ←/→ in a palette's workspace, which its step buttons name in `aria-keyshortcuts`, also never with shift (it extends a selection), in a tablist or a popover or for a held key's repeats, since a key held down repainted the whole page at the keyboard's repeat rate (a flashing risk, WCAG 2.3.1), and the landing's `uwu`, which counts only single characters, so a shift or an arrow between the letters no longer breaks or makes the word. The one chord, ⌘K or ctrl K on `/marketplace` (either modifier with K and nothing else; `letter` also takes the K key on a layout that types no Latin letter there), focuses the search and selects its text, outside a field and never while an IME composes; the field names `Meta+K Control+K` in `aria-keyshortcuts`, and its `Kbd` shows `⌘ k` on Apple's platforms and `ctrl k` elsewhere from the first paint: `KEYS_SCRIPT` (`lib/keys.ts`), inline in every head, marks `<html>` `data-apple` from the user agent, which `globals.css` reads to show one of the two and `client/copy.ts` to name ⌘c or ctrl+c. It was `/`, a single character, which WCAG 2.1.4 allows only with a way to turn it off or remap it, since speech input or a stray key fires it; `uwu`, a decoration, is the one single-character shortcut left. A search field is `autocapitalize="none"` and `autocorrect="off"`, since a mobile keyboard otherwise capitalizes or corrects `miku`.
- Names and commands carry `translate="no"` — the wordmarks, `CommandRow`'s code, `TerminalPreview`, the landing's name, window title and character tiles, and the picker's trigger, names and shelves — so a page translator leaves `ttheme use miku` and a palette named with a word (`determination`) alone; the attribute is inherited, so it goes on the element that holds the name, never on a section that also holds prose. A work's native title carries its language wherever it shows — the work cards on `/marketplace`, a marketplace page's sections, a palette page's work — from `langOf` (`lib/theme-entry.ts`, the `lang` of a `WorkRef`): `ja` for kana, `ko` for Hangul, and `ja` for Han alone only for a work the commons holds, whose titles are all Japanese, since a marketplace's proposal could be Chinese; a Latin title (`serial experiments lain`) carries none. With it a screen reader switches to a Japanese voice and a fallback font draws Japanese rather than Chinese glyph forms (WCAG 3.1.2).
- Every terminal window the site draws follows Ghostty's macOS window, through one frame where it can: `TerminalFrame` (`components/terminal-frame.tsx`) — the landing's hero (`heading`, its title drawn from the page's vars, and `controls`, the cycle's buttons with `data-lead-progress` riding the bar's bottom edge), the landing's tab window (`tabs`: the `tab` `ToggleGroup`, its `+` a `scene` `Button` beside the track), the marketplace list's peek windows (`title`, `controls`), the marketplace workspace (`tabs`: the `tab` `ToggleGroup`) and the builder's mock window (`MockWindow` in `components/mock-window.tsx`: `tabs`, a `track` `TabsList` whose panes `palette-builder` switches, and `controls`, `EditMode`, which the frame draws after the tabs in its tabbed title bar too). So the builder's window wears the draft as the workspace's wears its palette — its title bar is the draft's background and its lights the draft's `--a1`, `--a3` and `--a2`, so an edit to one of them shows on the window too —, where the palette editor's mock in the terminal keeps fixed macOS lights in the terminal's own colors. The shape: 14px corners (`rounded-xl`), a `ring-border` hairline, a title bar in the terminal's own background with no line under it, 12px lights 8px apart, and tabs in a rounded track (`rounded-lg`, the window's foreground at 6%) whose selected tab is a lighter `rounded-md` block, each led by a `❯` that takes the window's `--a5` once selected, and whose others are split by short hairlines that hide beside it. Corners nest: the frame's 14px, the track's 10px set 5–6px inside it, a tab's 8px 2px inside that — keep them sharing a center when changing any. The `track` tabs and the `tab` toggles read the window's `--fg`, since every window that carries tabs wears another palette than the page.
- Printed, a page wears no palette: `@media print` sets `--bg`, `--fg`, `--cu` and `--se` to ink on white (`!important`, over the inline slots), `color-scheme: light` and `print-color-adjust: exact`, so the character tiles, chips and buttons keep their fills, and drops the facet ground, the route progress bar, the kaomoji rain and the cycle's line — a dark palette printed its text on white at about 3.3:1 (Chrome darkens it only that far) under the fixed facet canvas, which printed as a full-page dark image.
- KitaJS escapes attributes but not children: a string child takes `safe` on its element, or `escapeHtml()` when it is a component's child, and `xss-scan` (`@kitajs/ts-html-plugin`, run by `site:build`) fails otherwise — marketplace data comes from anyone's repository. Both KitaJS packages are pinned to their 5.0 prereleases, since 4.x's `xss-scan` exited 0 when it crashed and `site:build` passed unscanned; 5.0 needs TypeScript 6, so `site/` stays on 6 and Dependabot skips TypeScript's majors there until the plugin reads 7's API. An `aria-*` that must read `false` is written `String(value)`, since KitaJS drops a `false` attribute.
- Tokens use shadcn's vocabulary plus `soft-foreground`, `faint-foreground`, `success`, `warning`, `overlay`, `glass`, `glass-panel`, `raised` (with `raised-low`, `raised-edge` and `raised-shine`) and `star`: there is no second light or dark theme, only the mode the worn palette is shown in (the mode bullet below): `:root` itself wears a palette — `globals.css` maps every token onto the slots `--bg`, `--fg`, `--cu`, `--se` and `--a0`–`--a15`, `<html style>` sets them from the site's first palette (`fallback`, `lib/builtin.ts`: `ttheme/miku`, `FIRST` in `lib/works.ts`, which `leadsOf` puts before the other leads in work-name order) and `WEAR_SCRIPT` (`wearScript` in `lib/wear.ts`, built from the first lead so it can wear that lead's light shade too), inline in the document's head, overrides them before first paint with the visitor's stored pick in the mode the visitor chose — so every page wears the palette chosen in the header. Shadows are the dark `--elevation-*` ones, and softer ones under `:root[data-mode='light']`, since a 70% black lip on a pale ground reads dirty. Text selection is the palette's own selection slot under its foreground (`::selection` reads `--se` and `--fg` where the selected text sits, so inside a preview window that sets its own slots it is that palette's; a text fragment's highlight, `::target-text`, the same), a field's caret is its cursor color, `<html>`'s `color-scheme` is the mode worn, `light only` or `dark only` (`rootStyle`, `WEAR_SCRIPT`, `show()` and the hand-off, which reads it with `includes`, since Chromium serializes `only` last), so form controls, the scrollbars and system colors follow the palette and no browser's automatic dark theme (Chrome's dark theme for sites) inverts a light one; the scrollbars' thumb is the palette's `--faint-foreground` on a transparent track (`scrollbar-color` on `html`, which every scroller inherits, but the builder's sidebar, which keeps a thin `--border` one, and which forced colors set back to `auto`), and `<meta name="theme-color">` carries the worn background — the server writes it, `WEAR_SCRIPT` sets the stored pick's, `HANDOFF_SCRIPT` the colors a page arrives in, and the slot fade writes it every frame — so a browser that tints its toolbar from it wears the palette too. The mark is `>ω`, one source for every drawing of it, the CLI's too (`src/core/mark.ts`: `STROKES`, a winking eye and a mouth on a 64 grid, `pathOf` for SVG and `pointsOf` for the PNG icons' own rasterizer): the eye's lower arm in signature 3, drawn first and wrapping the eye's round corner, its upper arm in signature 2, a straight stroke laid over it, so no round end sits on the corner, and the mouth in signature 1, each lifted toward the foreground in OKLab until it reads 3:1 on the background (`marksOf`; 24 of the 150 built-in palettes have a signature color under 3:1 in each mode, measured 2026-10-21), never animated; on a tile its center is `CENTER`, halfway between the glyph's box and its ink's center of mass, since the thick eye pulls the ink left and down. The page wears it as CSS variables, `--mark0`–`--mark2`, which are derived and never stored: `withMarks` adds them to `rootStyle`'s and `show()`'s vars, `WEAR_SCRIPT` computes them from the stored pick's `--bg`, `--fg` and `--sg0`–`--sg2` with its own copy of the lift (`LIFT`), and `worn()` carries them through a handoff. The header's and footer's wordmark draw it before the word (`components/mark.tsx` inside a `data-slot='lockup'`, which `globals.css` sets 0.75em high and 0.3em from the word, so it follows the header's folding font size, in those variables). The word is a drawing, not text: `lib/wordmark.ts` holds `ttheme` as one path of Nunito 900's own outlines at −0.01em (`WORDMARK`), with the second t moved 50 units right and the two crossbars joined into one bar under nonzero fill — Nunito has no tt ligature, and the kerned text left the bars touching only at their round ends, pinched above and below, with the first t's tail 33 units from the second stem —, sized by the line box a line of Nunito takes at a given leading (`lineBox`, from its ascent and descent), so it sits where the text sat; `Wordmark` (`components/wordmark.tsx`, `role="img"`) draws it in the header, the footer and the landing footer, and `wordmarkSvg` on the cards and the README's banner, and the tab icon wears it as well (`wearIcon`, `lib/wear.ts`): the worn background as a tile (`rx` 12 of 64) under a hairline of the foreground at 22%, with the mark at 0.92, a `data:` SVG that reads on light and dark tab strips alike. The server writes the page's, `WEAR_SCRIPT` the stored pick's (its own copy of the builder over the same `tileSvg` template, which gives the same bytes as `wearIcon`), and a palette fade swaps it once it settles, never frame by frame; a page arriving in another page's colors shows its own icon, as it shows its own title.

## Gotchas

- A page wrapper that is a `grid` needs `grid-cols-[minmax(0,1fr)]`, or a child's min-content (a nowrap command, an `auto-fit` grid) widens the track past a phone's width; a `<button>` used as a grid container needs it too.
- A row of parts that cannot shrink — labels, icon buttons, fixed-width cells — gives none of them `min-w-0`: a group with it shrinks below its content, which then paints past the group over its neighbor (the header's left group put `builder` under `github`), where without it the row overflows or, with `flex-wrap`, wraps; a part that can give way truncates instead (`min-w-0` with `truncate`, as the picker's name does). Such a row's breakpoints are its own width, a container query (the header's `site-header`), not the window's: `@media (width …)` counts a rendered scrollbar, so on a page with one the header's row was 15px narrower than its query said.
- `show()` fades `<html>`'s slots and the signature colors (`TONES`, `--sg0`–`--sg2`, so the picker's dots, the footer's keys and the sparkles follow the fade instead of snapping) over 0.4s on the view transitions' curve (`FADE`, `client/wear.ts`; at once under reduced motion) by writing each frame's mix itself — a `requestAnimationFrame` loop timed by a target-less `Animation`, so the easing is the browser's own, a palette picked mid-fade starts from the colors on screen, and a `show()` of the colors it is already fading to changes nothing — and holds `data-settling` on `<html>` from the fade's start to the frame after its last, which turns off every descendant's own transition, so text and borders follow the fade exactly instead of chasing it.
