# site/

## Conventions

- The design system is the Design System artifact https://claude.ai/artifact/D3TtwiZtWyM175ydwyWCyv — read its README before styling anything. `app/globals.css` is its code form.
- `site/` is an Elysia 2 app (`elysia` pinned to `2.0.0-beta.26` — a beta, so a bump is a deliberate step) that renders KitaJS JSX (`@kitajs/html`, the `jsxImportSource` in `tsconfig.json`) to strings: there is no React and no client framework. `app/site.tsx` holds the routes and the 404 and 500 handlers (`error('global', …)`, so they also answer paths no route matched), and every response goes through `page()` (`app/document.tsx`: the document, its status, its `cache-control` and an `etag` hashed from its bytes, which Vercel's CDN answers a browser's revalidation with as 304, a cache miss included) — a route passes its `path`, and a page with one carries `<link rel="canonical">` and `og:url` on the production origin (`SHARE_URL`'s, `src/core/own.ts`), so a preview deployment or the git-branch alias never stands in for it, a route passes its `ld` graphs, which `Document` writes as JSON-LD (`lib/seo.ts`: the landing's `WebSite` and `SoftwareSourceCode` — not `SoftwareApplication`, whose rich result wants ratings or reviews ttheme does not collect, so Search Console would list the landing as an invalid item, and `crumbs` for a marketplace's page and a palette's), and its `robots` — `max-image-preview:large` unless the route says otherwise, and `noindex` for a share link, which never gets a `Disallow: /p/` in `robots.txt` because a link preview's crawler obeys that too and would stop reading the card the link exists for; every page's title and description come from `lib/titles.ts`, so each prerendered page has its own (`paletteMeta`'s title is also what `client/marketplace-workspace.tsx` sets as `document.title` when a palette changes in place); every page carries `og:type`, `og:site_name`, `og:locale` and a `twitter:card` (`summary`, or `summary_large_image` with a card, whose `og:image:type`, `og:image:alt` and `twitter:image:alt` name the palette) and links `/apple-touch-icon.png`; a route passes its card as a path, which `Document` puts on the production origin too. `og:image` carries `?v=` and `CARD_REV` (`lib/seo.ts`: the version and an `embed.ts` macro's hash of the renderer's sources), since Discord, Slack and X keep an image by its URL for days and a card redrawn at the same address kept showing the old one. Every page a visitor shares has a card (`lib/card.ts`, below): a palette page its own at its path plus `/card.png`, a marketplace's page its own at its path plus `/card.png`, a share link its code's, and the landing, `/sheets` and `/marketplace` `/card.png`, the first lead's (`fallback`); the 404 and 500 pages have none. `/apple-touch-icon.png` (`touchIconPng` in `lib/tab-icon.ts`, 180×180) is the tab icon's three signature dots on the first lead's background, opaque and square, since iOS rounds a home-screen icon itself and takes none from an SVG `rel=icon`; `/favicon.ico` (`faviconIco`, the same dots as a 48×48 PNG in an ICO container) is for a crawler that does not read the page's data-URI `rel=icon`, and `/robots.txt` allows every path and names `/sitemap.xml` (`sitemapXml`: a `loc` for every `PRERENDERED` path and no `lastmod`, since a page rebuilt on every deploy has no date worth saying); the cards and the icons come from the function, cached at the CDN like the pages. A card is one SVG string that `raster()` (`lib/raster.ts`, `@resvg/resvg-wasm`) turns into a PNG: the page's terminal window, wearing the palette, on its own facet ground (`facets` over `lib/facets.ts`' `shade` at half strength, mixed in OKLab by `lib/oklab.ts`), the name in the cursor color at the left — two rows, split at a `-` or an `@`, when one row would come out under 60px —, its catalog (the marketplace id for a palette with none, `shared palette` for a share link), the three signature dots, and in the window the shell scene and the sixteen ANSI colors; a marketplace's card lists its first catalog leads in the window, and the site card's `HEADLINE` — `wear your favorite` over `anime character`, `in your terminal` under it — is the one wording the user chose. `raster()` embeds its own wasm and fonts: the macros in `lib/embed.ts` run when a module is transpiled or bundled and inline the resvg wasm, Nunito 900, Inter 500 and JetBrains Mono 400 and 700 (subset to sfnt with `subset-font`, since resvg reads no woff2) and a BIZ UDGothic piece for the `☆` of `Lucky☆Star`, which resvg finds by falling back glyph by glyph through the fonts it holds, so the bundle reads no file and runs from a folder with no `node_modules`; a font's family is read from its `name` table and never typed, since a Fontsource static Nunito calls itself `Nunito ExtraLight Black` and a pinned Inter instance keeps the family and weight of its variable font, and a text is measured by rendering it (`ink`, `fit`: resvg's `getBBox`), which also aligns a big name by its ink and not by its advance. A character the fonts lack draws as nothing. Measured 2026-10-12 on the bundle alone: about 110 ms a card, a first render about 200 ms, the process holding at about 300 MB through 298 cards and 24 at once, and `server.js` growing from 1.25 to 2.72 MB. `lib/tab-icon.ts` keeps the two icons on the pure-TypeScript `encodeRgb`, and `site/lib/tab-icon.test.ts` holds that a rectangle with no corner is painted whole, which it once was not (a half-bright background on every card and on the touch icon). `@elysia/html` is not used because its `mapResponse` is plugin-scoped and never reaches the outer app's 404 and 500. `server.ts` is Vercel's entry (`export default`; Vercel refuses `listen`), `dev.ts` the local server; both `use(autoHead())` (`elysia/auto-head`) first, since Elysia 2 answers `HEAD` only through it, and without it every page and the `og:image` answered a link preview's or a monitor's `HEAD` with 404.
- UI parts live in `components/ui/*`: KitaJS components whose looks are `cva` variants in their own file, never a long `class` passed from a feature component. Every part renders `data-slot` (and `data-variant`/`data-size`) and carries no `dark:` variants — tokens do the theming. Behavior comes from native elements, not a library: `Dialog` is a `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()` (focus trap, Esc, `::backdrop`; `:root:has(dialog:modal)` locks the page's scroll), a `ToggleGroup` is a radio group (`has-checked:` styles, arrow keys for free; its `underline` variant draws tabs over a hairline as wide as their row, the checked one underlined in `primary`, and `ToggleGroupCount` puts a count pill in a tab — `/marketplace`'s source tabs), `TabsList` a `role="tablist"` whose keys `client/tabs.ts` moves, `ScrollArea` a native overflow box. A toggle that only picks which panel shows needs no script: the marketplace workspace's install step and scene are radio groups whose section is a named `group`, and each panel is `hidden` with a `group-has-[input[value=…]:checked]/<group>:` variant, spelled out per value since Tailwind only generates classes it finds whole in the source. Icons are `lucide` nodes drawn by `Icon` (`lib/icons.tsx`), and a brand wears its own mark: `simple-icons` (CC0, each mark taken from the brand's published logo) drawn by `BrandIcon`, filled with `currentColor` and set before the brand's name — GitHub on the header's and footer's links and every repository line, npm in the footer, and pixiv, X, Bluesky and Patreon on an artist's links (`PictureLink.kind`, which `lib/credits.ts` fills from `linksOf` and `pageKind`); running text keeps the word, and a brand it does not carry stays a word. A link off the site opens in a new tab (`target="_blank" rel="noopener"`, without `noreferrer`, so GitHub and npm still see where a visit came from) and ends in `NewTab` (`lib/icons.tsx`): Lucide's `ArrowUpRight`, sized like the brand mark beside it, and `, opens in a new tab` for a screen reader — in the footer inside the label's `span` (`OutLabel`), since `[data-glide]` anchors its underline on every `span` a link holds directly, and a second one would pull the underline onto the hidden text, with the label's last word and the arrow in one `whitespace-nowrap` span, since a line may break before any inline SVG and a label that wraps in the narrow columns would leave the arrow alone on a line. `simple-icons` is one 5.3 MB module: only the marks a page imports reach a bundle (`sideEffects: false`), but an inline source map would carry all of it, so the dev build links its map (`main.js.map`) instead of inlining it.
- Interactivity is vanilla TypeScript in `client/`, bundled by `Bun.build` into one module: each page's widget is a custom element (`lead-showcase`, `facet-ground`, `route-progress`, `sheet-browser`, `share-sheet`, `marketplace-board`, `marketplace-workspace`, `palette-picker`, `sparkle-burst`, `kaomoji-rain`; declared for JSX in `custom-elements.d.ts`) that reads its data from a `<script type="application/json">` the server wrote with `json()` (`lib/render.ts`, which escapes `<`, `>` and `&`), and re-renders a changed fragment with the same KitaJS component the server used (`html()`, then `fill`/`swap` from `client/dom.ts`), so every piece of markup has one implementation. A copy button is any `[data-copy]` button, handled by one delegated listener (`client/copy.ts`): it keeps its label, says `copied` through the page's one polite live region (`[data-announce]`, a `role="status"` that `Document` renders empty at the end of `<body>`, so a change in it is announced, written by `announce()` in `client/dom.ts`) — a filter that narrows a list says how many palettes it left there too, through `tally()` half a second after the last keystroke (the palette picker, `/marketplace`'s search and source tabs, the workspace's sidebar search), and a previous or next button names where it goes (`stepLabel` on the sheets page, `previous palette, <name>` in the workspace) while the visible note (`copied (・ω・)`) is `aria-hidden`, and when the clipboard refuses — no secure context, an in-app browser without the permission, a denied one — it selects the command's text (or the button's) and says to press ⌘c (on Apple's platforms, `data-apple`) or ctrl+c, three times as long. A command's `<code>` selects as a whole (`select-all`), so a double-click takes all of `npx @kecan0406/ttheme init` rather than one word of it.
- Every page has one `<main id="content">` — the landing's, `/sheets`' and the 404's content wrapper, `MarketplaceFrame`'s, the marketplace workspace's right pane and the share page's stage — and `Document` puts a `skip to content` link to it first in `<body>`: a `primary` pill fixed at the top left, above the header, that stays `sr-only` until it has focus (`not-focus:sr-only`), so the first Tab on any page offers it and Enter sends the next Tab into the page past the header. `html`'s `scroll-padding-top` (64px: the 52px the stuck header leaves on screen and a gap) keeps whatever the browser scrolls into view — a focused element, the link's or an anchor's target — clear of the header (WCAG 2.4.11): measured 2026-10-11, Shift+Tab back through the landing's character tiles left each row at the window's top edge, under the header's glass, and now leaves it 64px down.
- In forced colors (Windows' contrast themes) the browser drops every `box-shadow` and paints every author background `Canvas`, keeping only system colors, so the `ring` focus styles, the glide underline, a checked `ToggleGroup` item's or character tile's ring and the landing's progress line all vanished — measured 2026-10-11 under Chrome's forced-colors emulation: no `Button`, wordmark or picker trigger showed keyboard focus. `globals.css` draws it there as a 2px `Highlight` outline, on the focused element or on the label around a visually hidden radio (`:has(> .sr-only:focus-visible)`), underlines a checked one's label in `Highlight` and paints the underline and the progress line `Highlight` (`!important` against the footer's `--key` rules); a system color survives forced colors with no `forced-color-adjust`. A swatch — the signature dots (`Signature`, `WornSignature`, the picker trigger's), `Swatch`, the landing's and the footer's ANSI pills and ribbon, and the one-element chips (`signature-dots`, `catalog-tile`, `catalog-swatch`, `palette-dots`) — takes `forced-color-adjust: none`, since its color is the content and forced colors painted it `Canvas`; nothing that holds text does. `prefers-contrast: more` lifts the muted, soft and faint foregrounds and the borders toward the foreground and makes the glass nearly opaque, and `prefers-reduced-transparency` makes it opaque.
- A page's keyboard shortcuts go through `client/keys.ts`. A single key never fires with ⌘, ctrl or alt held (Alt+← and ⌘+← are the browser's Back), for a key the page already handled (`defaultPrevented`), in a field (`typing`: input, textarea, select, contenteditable) or during an IME composition (`composing`): ←/→ on `/sheets`, which its step buttons name in `aria-keyshortcuts`, also never with shift (it extends a selection), in a tablist or a popover or for a held key's repeats, since a key held down repainted the whole page at the keyboard's repeat rate (a flashing risk, WCAG 2.3.1), and the landing's `uwu`, which counts only single characters, so a shift or an arrow between the letters no longer breaks or makes the word. The one chord, ⌘K or ctrl K on `/marketplace` (either modifier with K and nothing else; `letter` also takes the K key on a layout that types no Latin letter there), focuses the search and selects its text, outside a field and never while an IME composes; the field names `Meta+K Control+K` in `aria-keyshortcuts`, and its `Kbd` shows `⌘ k` on Apple's platforms and `ctrl k` elsewhere from the first paint: `KEYS_SCRIPT` (`lib/keys.ts`), inline in every head, marks `<html>` `data-apple` from the user agent, which `globals.css` reads to show one of the two and `client/copy.ts` to name ⌘c or ctrl+c. It was `/`, a single character, which WCAG 2.1.4 allows only with a way to turn it off or remap it, since speech input or a stray key fires it; `uwu`, a decoration, is the one single-character shortcut left. A search field is `autocapitalize="none"` and `autocorrect="off"`, since a mobile keyboard otherwise capitalizes or corrects `miku`.
- Names and commands carry `translate="no"` — the wordmarks, `CommandRow`'s code, `TerminalPreview`, the landing's name, window title and character tiles, and the picker's trigger, names and shelves — so a page translator leaves `ttheme use miku` and a palette named with a word (`determination`) alone; the attribute is inherited, so it goes on the element that holds the name, never on a section that also holds prose. A catalog's native title carries its language wherever it shows — the catalog cards on `/marketplace`, a marketplace page's sections, a palette page's catalog — from `nativeLang` (`lib/catalogs.ts`): `ja` for kana, `ko` for Hangul, and `ja` for Han alone only in the official marketplace, whose titles are all Japanese, since another marketplace's could be Chinese; a Latin title (`serial experiments lain`) carries none. With it a screen reader switches to a Japanese voice and a fallback font draws Japanese rather than Chinese glyph forms (WCAG 3.1.2).
- Every terminal window the site draws follows Ghostty's macOS window, through one frame where it can: `TerminalFrame` (`components/terminal-frame.tsx`) — the landing's hero (`heading`, its title drawn from the page's vars, and `controls`, the cycle's buttons with `data-lead-progress` riding the bar's bottom edge), `/sheets`' `TerminalWindow` (`tabs`: `TerminalTabs` on the `track` `TabsList` and `TabsTrigger` variants, with `+` as a round outline button outside the track), the marketplace list's peek windows (`title`, `controls`) and the marketplace workspace (`tabs`: the `tab` `ToggleGroup`). The share page's mock (`.mock`) keeps its own markup, since it is the palette editor's mock window and keeps that window's fixed macOS lights, but takes the same shape. The shape: 14px corners (`rounded-xl`, `.mock`'s `--radius × 1.4`), a `border` hairline, a title bar with no line under it (the frame's is the terminal's own background; the mock's stays `card`, so the draft never colors the window), 12px lights 8px apart, and tabs in a rounded track (`rounded-lg`, the foreground at 6%) whose selected tab is a lighter `rounded-md` block and whose others are split by short hairlines that hide beside it. Corners nest: the frame's 14px, the track's 10px set 5–6px inside it, a tab's 8px 2px inside that — keep them sharing a center when changing any. The `track` tabs read the site's `foreground` and the workspace's `tab` toggles the window's `--fg`, since only the workspace's window wears another palette than the page.
- Printed, a page wears no palette: `@media print` sets `--bg`, `--fg`, `--cu` and `--se` to ink on white (`!important`, over the inline slots), `color-scheme: light` and `print-color-adjust: exact`, so the character tiles, chips and buttons keep their fills, and drops the facet ground, the route progress bar, the kaomoji rain and the cycle's line — a dark palette printed its text on white at about 3.3:1 (Chrome darkens it only that far) under the fixed facet canvas, which printed as a full-page dark image.
- KitaJS escapes attributes but not children: a string child takes `safe` on its element, or `escapeHtml()` when it is a component's child, and `xss-scan` (`@kitajs/ts-html-plugin`, run by `site:build`) fails otherwise — marketplace data comes from anyone's repository. Both KitaJS packages are pinned to their 5.0 prereleases, since 4.x's `xss-scan` exited 0 when it crashed and `site:build` passed unscanned; 5.0 needs TypeScript 6, so `site/` stays on 6 and Dependabot skips TypeScript's majors there until the plugin reads 7's API. An `aria-*` that must read `false` is written `String(value)`, since KitaJS drops a `false` attribute.
- Tokens use shadcn's vocabulary plus `soft-foreground`, `faint-foreground`, `success`, `warning`, `overlay`, `glass`, `glass-panel`: there is no second light or dark theme, only the mode the worn palette is shown in (the mode bullet below): `:root` itself wears a palette — `globals.css` maps every token onto the slots `--bg`, `--fg`, `--cu`, `--se` and `--a0`–`--a15`, `<html style>` sets them from the first lead (`fallback`, `lib/official.ts`) and `WEAR_SCRIPT` (`wearScript` in `lib/wear.ts`, built from the first lead so it can wear that lead's light shade too), inline in the document's head, overrides them before first paint with the visitor's stored pick in the mode the visitor chose — so every page wears the palette chosen in the header. Shadows are the dark `--elevation-*` ones, and softer ones under `:root[data-mode='light']`, since a 70% black lip on a pale ground reads dirty. Text selection is the palette's own selection slot under its foreground (`::selection` reads `--se` and `--fg` where the selected text sits, so inside a preview window that sets its own slots it is that palette's; a text fragment's highlight, `::target-text`, the same), a field's caret is its cursor color, `<html>`'s `color-scheme` is the mode worn, `light only` or `dark only` (`rootStyle`, `WEAR_SCRIPT`, `show()` and the hand-off, which reads it with `includes`, since Chromium serializes `only` last), so form controls, the scrollbars and system colors follow the palette and no browser's automatic dark theme (Chrome's dark theme for sites) inverts a light one; the scrollbars' thumb is the palette's `--faint-foreground` on a transparent track (`scrollbar-color` on `html`, which every scroller inherits, but `ScrollArea` and the share page's sidebar, which keep a thin `--border` one, and which forced colors set back to `auto`), and `<meta name="theme-color">` carries the worn background — the server writes it, `WEAR_SCRIPT` sets the stored pick's, `HANDOFF_SCRIPT` the colors a page arrives in, and the slot fade writes it every frame — so a browser that tints its toolbar from it wears the palette too. The tab icon wears it as well (`wearIcon`, `lib/wear.ts`): the worn background as a 16px tile (`rx` 4) under a hairline of the foreground at 22%, with the three signature dots in a row, a `data:` SVG that reads on light and dark tab strips alike; there is no fixed mark. The server writes the page's, `WEAR_SCRIPT` the stored pick's (its own copy of the builder, which gives the same bytes as `wearIcon` for every official palette), and a palette fade swaps it once it settles, never frame by frame; a page arriving in another page's colors shows its own icon, as it shows its own title.
- Only the scale: text `2xs` 11 · `xs` 12 · `code` 12.5 · `sm` 13 · `base` 15 · `display-sm/md/lg` (fluid) and `display-xl` for the landing's hero only and `mega` for the landing footer's wordmark only, radius from `--radius: 10px` (sm 6 · md 8 · lg 10 · xl 14 · 2xl 18 · 3xl 22), spacing on Tailwind's 4px steps. `text-[13.5px]`, `gap-[9px]`, `rounded-[3px]` are off the system; arbitrary values are for layout tracks, breakpoints and blur only.
- Faces: `font-sans` Inter for UI, `font-display` `Nunito` → `Hiragino Maru Gothic ProN` → `BIZ UDGothic` for palette names, the wordmark and page titles at `font-black` (list rows `font-extrabold`), `font-mono` JetBrains Mono for commands, ids, hex values and terminals. Inter, Nunito and BIZ UDGothic come from Fontsource and JetBrains Mono from `app/fonts`, which `build.ts` cuts with `subset-font` into `unicode-range` blocks (`MONO_SUBSETS`: `core` — latin and the dingbats every mock terminal draws, ❯ ✓ ✗ ➜ —, arrows, math, technical, shapes, and `rest` for everything else), so a page downloads only the blocks its text uses — 72–80 KB of mono on each page instead of the whole 187 KB. Every font goes to `public/assets` under a content hash (woff2 only, never inlined into the CSS), its `@font-face` minified with the rest of the stylesheet, and only the latin Inter, Nunito 900 and the regular JetBrains Mono `core` are preloaded (preloading the bold `core` too moved LCP later on `/marketplace` and `/p/<code>`). BIZ UDGothic is the display stack's last fallback, reached only by Japanese and a few symbols (the kaomoji, ✦), and its 248 faces were two thirds of the stylesheet, so they live in their own `cjk` stylesheet that loads without blocking render (`media="print"` until it loads). Each face has a `* Fallback` face in `globals.css` carrying next/font's metric overrides, so nothing shifts when it loads.
- Every page, the landing included, lies on the facet ground (`components/facet-ground.tsx`, which `Document` puts in `<body>` right after the route progress bar, `client/facet-ground.ts`, `lib/facets.ts`): a fixed field of low-poly triangles behind the page whose every color comes from one function, `shade` (`lib/facets.ts`: a facet's mix toward one of the signature colors, 6–16% and more near the lamp, and an OKLab lightness lift from a point lamp above the field — its diffuse light, a broad glint and a glow around it), drawn twice from it. The server writes the field as SVG with each facet's `--tint` and `--lift` for the resting lamp (`FACET_LAMP`, the upper left), which CSS turns into `oklab(from color-mix(…) calc(l + var(--lift)) a b)` on the slots, so the first paint is right before any script runs; then `client/facet-ground.ts` draws the same field on a canvas with the same function and the same OKLab math (within 5/255 of the SVG) and takes the SVG out of the DOM. That swap is the point: an inline SVG of hundreds of shapes made every document-wide restyle cost about 24 µs a shape, and the palette fade restyles the whole document each frame, so with the SVG in the DOM a palette change ran at 17 fps (45–55 ms a frame) and with the canvas at 60 (about 5 ms) — never move per-facet work back into DOM elements or CSS. The canvas holds at most 2× and 8 M pixels, and a scroll-driven animation drifts it (and the SVG before it) at a fifth of the scroll. The pointer is the lamp: a mouse or pen move eases it toward the pointer (120 ms) and raises the mesh under it (200 ms), so each facet catches or loses the light by its own slope, and the frame loop runs only while the lamp moves or a facet turns, about 1–2 ms a frame. When `show()` dispatches `SHOW_EVENT` (`Shown`: the vars it wears, an origin, `instant`) the field turns over in a wave from the origin (the press that left the page before, for a page arriving in its colors), the last press, the palette on show (the landing's preview, the sheets page's session, the marketplace workspace's preview pane, `[data-palette-preview]`) or the middle: each facet waits its distance at 2 units a millisecond, then folds along the wave's direction for 360 ms, eased, its back face the new palette, over its new color in shadow; a facet still turning when another palette comes finishes first, and one an earlier wave never reached turns once, to the latest. The field knows a palette by the colors it draws with (the background and three tones, normalized), never by its name, so a `SHOW_EVENT` for the colors it shows or turns toward does nothing, and an `instant` one redraws it at once. Under reduced motion the field is drawn still and changes at once, and a touch pointer lights nothing. Every page but the landing stays at this soft level and nothing louder: cards and the dialog `rounded-3xl` (22px), buttons `rounded-full`, inputs and command rows `rounded-lg`; neutrals are mixes of the worn palette; `ease-spring` (a `linear()` spring with a `cubic-bezier` fallback) only on a card's `animate-enter` and press, never on hover; nav, labels and toggles lowercase; a kaomoji only in a passing state (`copied (・ω・)`, an empty result, no such marketplace), one per view, never in a heading, doc or error, and always `aria-hidden`, since a screen reader spells one out symbol by symbol.
- Every page has the route progress bar, `route-progress` (`client/route-progress.ts`, `lib/route-progress.ts`, its rules in `globals.css`), which `Document` puts first in `<body>` so it exists in the first frame: a 3px bar in `primary` along the top edge with no glow (nothing else casts a shadow or is a gradient), a `role="progressbar"` labelled `loading` that is `visibility: hidden` while idle. It follows the Navigation API's `navigate` event (Baseline since 2026-01; without it nothing draws): every navigation that leaves the document for a page of this origin — a link, back/forward, a script's — and none that stays (a hash, `pushState`), downloads a file or goes to another origin. It moves only `opacity` and `translate`, so it runs on the compositor, and JS only sets `data-state`: `going` waits `--route-delay` (200ms, so a navigation the CDN answers at once never draws it), fades in and drifts from 8% toward 95% over 10s on one `linear()` curve (`route-drift`) — never drive it through a custom property animation, which recalculates style on the main thread every frame. A stop, a newer navigation or a canceled event aborts the event's `signal`, and `stopped` freezes the bar where it is and fades it out — one task later, since Chromium aborts the old event and dispatches the newer navigation's in the same task, and that one keeps the bar going on the same timeline. Chromium drops a 204 or a server-sent download without aborting anything, so 10s after the last navigation (`LIMIT_MS`) `done` completes it. The next document finishes the bar: at `navigate` the old page writes `{ at, nav }` (when the bar's timeline began, when this navigation did) to `sessionStorage` under `ROUTE_KEY` — at the start, never at `pagehide`, which can run after the next document has read it — and `ROUTE_SCRIPT`, inline in `<head>` on every page, takes it only when `Date.now() - nav` is within 500ms of `performance.now()` (both the time since the click; `performance.timeOrigin` is not on `Date.now()`'s clock), so a reload, the address bar or a stale entry shows nothing, and writes `--route-from` (ms since the timeline began) and `data-arriving` on `<html>` before first paint. `route-in` and `route-drift` then sit paused at a delay of `--route-delay` minus `--route-from`, so the browser puts the bar where the old page left it from the same keyframes, and `route-done`, which has no `from` keyframe and so starts from the paused drift beneath it, runs it to 100% before `route-out` fades it (measured 2026-10-10: the old page's last frame 0.480, the new page's first 0.493 at 1598ms, the curve's 0.493); a navigation shorter than the delay arrives with the bar still waiting, so it never shows. `animationend` of `route-out` returns it to idle (`connectedCallback` does when the arrival ended before the module ran). A page restored from the back/forward cache, frozen mid-drift, drops the bar at once on `pageshow`; nothing changes at `pagehide`, since the old page can still draw a frame after it, which paint holding could show between the two documents.

## Gotchas

- A page wrapper that is a `grid` needs `grid-cols-[minmax(0,1fr)]`, or a child's min-content (a nowrap command, an `auto-fit` grid) widens the track past a phone's width; a `<button>` used as a grid container needs it too.
- `show()` fades `<html>`'s slots and the signature colors (`TONES`, `--sg0`–`--sg2`, so the picker's dots, the footer's keys and the sparkles follow the fade instead of snapping) over 0.4s on the view transitions' curve (`FADE`, `client/wear.ts`; at once under reduced motion) by writing each frame's mix itself — a `requestAnimationFrame` loop timed by a target-less `Animation`, so the easing is the browser's own, a palette picked mid-fade starts from the colors on screen, and a `show()` of the colors it is already fading to changes nothing — and holds `data-settling` on `<html>` from the fade's start to the frame after its last, which turns off every descendant's own transition, so text and borders follow the fade exactly instead of chasing it. Never fade the slots with a CSS transition or animation: Chromium marks an animated custom property's value animation-tainted, every token built from it carries the taint, and it refuses a tainted `var()` in every `-internal-*` property, `-internal-visited-color` among them — the color it paints a visited link's text with — which then takes the parent's color; so while the slots transitioned, every visited link drew its parent's text color (measured 2026-10-11: the landing's `browse the palettes` turned the near-white `--fg` on its `--cu` pill and the header's `sheets` and `marketplace` brightened from `--soft-foreground` to `--fg`, while an unvisited copy kept its colors; backgrounds escape only because Blink falls back to the unvisited background when the visited one comes out transparent). A TerminalPreview inside a worn page takes `worn` and inherits the slots rather than setting them, or it snaps while the page fades. The change itself runs inside `fadeThrough()` (`client/dom.ts`, one `document.startViewTransition`): every fragment whose text the palette changes — `lead-showcase`'s name, title and command, `sheet-browser`'s heading, session pane, command, the details' two columns and the selected tab's label — carries a `view-transition-name` while `fadeThrough` runs (`globals.css`, scoped to its element under `:root[data-fading]`, which `fadeThrough` sets before the transition starts and the newest one takes off once it finishes; the tab's comes from `--tab-name`, inline and keyed by its id, so selecting another tab never slides a label across) — named all the time, they would join every cross-document transition as well, the landing's name fading out over the page that comes next — and leaves in its old colors within 0.12s before the new one fades in over a palette already three quarters arrived, so no frame shows new text in the old colors or two texts over each other. The landing's preview is not captured: its lines are the same for every palette but the name in its `ttheme use` line, so they stay and only recolor with the slots while that name changes at once — captured, every line faded out and back in for one changed word. Nothing else is captured: `:root` takes `view-transition-name: none` and `::view-transition` `pointer-events: none`, since a captured element takes no clicks while it animates and a captured root or page wrapper swallowed every click while it ran. The transition layer draws above the whole page, so `fadeThrough` clips it (`--clear-top`, `--clear-bottom`) to the band between the `[data-sticky]` bars, which a fragment passing beneath them would otherwise cover, and a fragment a rounded ancestor clips (`sheet-session`) gets that radius on its group. The update runs a frame later, so it reads the state when it runs, and a change while one runs skips that one to its end. Without the API or under reduced motion the text changes at once. The header lies outside that band, so the picker's name (`[data-wear-label]`, drawn from `--wear-name`) cross-fades on the same timing by itself: `relabel()` keeps the old name in the label's `--wear-was`, which its `::before` draws over the new one, and `data-renaming` runs the view transitions' own `fade-out` and `fade-in` on the two (a later rename cancels and replays them).
- Every swatch that stands for a palette is one element: the workspace's signature dots (`signature-dots`: `--sg0`–`--sg2` on itself, the first dot the element and the other two its `box-shadow` copies) and catalog tiles (`catalog-tile`: `--bg` and the three signs, the middle dot its `::before` and the outer two copies of it), and the list's catalog swatches (`catalog-swatch`: `--bg` and `--sg0`–`--sg2`) and palettes' dots (`palette-dots`: `--dot0`–`--dot8`, a copy past the last palette `transparent`). A palette chip drawn as nineteen `<i>` in two rows made three quarters of the elements on the marketplace pages (`/marketplace` with every row open: 6,000 → 1,485) and a quarter of the old `/marketplace/official`'s rendering (measured 2026-10-11: 64.7 → 48.5ms on desktop), so never draw a swatch with an element per color again. A copy can land on a half device pixel at 1x, which softens that dot's edge where elements snapped its width to whole pixels.
- Never put `content-visibility: auto` on rows, sheets or any list a visitor jumps through: Chrome unlocks a skipped element only after the frame that brought it into view, so a jump (the scrollbar, End, an anchor, `scrollTo`) drew every row in view empty for two frames (measured 2026-10-11 on `/marketplace/official` and the `/sheets` filmstrip). It cut that page's rendering from 64 to 45ms on desktop, which a blank flash does not buy. Its `contain-intrinsic-size` is the content box, without padding and border, too.
- The head's inline scripts (`KEYS_SCRIPT`, `WEAR_SCRIPT`, `HANDOFF_SCRIPT`, `ROUTE_SCRIPT`) are classic scripts sharing one global scope: a top-level `var` is a property of `window` that a later script's `var` of the same name overwrites, and their listeners run after all of them. Keep a script's working variables inside its functions — a top-level `s` meant as `<html>`'s style became `ROUTE_SCRIPT`'s `<html>` element, and the hand-off threw inside its `try` without a word.
- `cn` (`lib/utils.ts`) extends tailwind-merge with the custom text sizes (`2xs`, `code`, `display-*`, `mega`). Add any new `--text-*` there, or tailwind-merge takes it for a color and drops it when a `text-<color>` follows.
- Two TypeScript programs: `tsconfig.json` (the server and the shared code: `esnext` and Bun's types, no DOM) and `client/tsconfig.json` (the DOM, no Bun). Client code never imports `lib/official.ts`, `lib/marketplaces.ts` or the root's `src/` — a type-only import still pulls a module into the program — and shared code (`components/`, `lib/`) touches neither `document` nor `Bun`.
- `xss-scan` takes `.map()` over an array that came from a JSON import for unescaped content; give the array a typed constant first (`FONTS` in `app/document.tsx`).
- JetBrains Mono's subsets keep every OpenType feature (`subset-font`'s default): `body`'s `font-feature-settings` (`cv02`, `cv03`, `cv04`, `cv11`, `ss01`, meant for Inter) is inherited by the mono text too, and JetBrains Mono has those features, so a subset that dropped them would draw other glyphs (`l.cv01`, `t.cv02`).
- The mono subsets cover every code point between them, with no catch-all face holding the whole font: a character the font lacks (☆ in `Lucky☆Star`) falls through every face whose range claims it, and a catch-all face is fetched whole just to find the glyph missing.
- The dev server's reload stream (`/__reload`, `RELOAD` in `app/document.tsx`) holds an HTTP/1.1 connection, and Chrome opens at most six to one host: when every page held its stream, the pages Chrome keeps beyond the one on screen — the back/forward cache, prerenders, background tabs — held theirs too, so after a few page changes six streams took every socket and the next page's request waited for one forever (measured 2026-10-11: the seventh new document never loaded, with seven `/__reload` requests open; production, on HTTP/2 and without the stream, never stalled). So a page holds its stream only while it is visible or prerendering — it closes it at `pagehide` and when hidden, and opens it again when shown — and the server sends its build stamp (`stamp` in `dev.ts`, new at each rebuild and each start) when a stream opens and after each rebuild; a page reloads when a stamp differs from the first it heard, so a page restored from the cache, or shown again, after a rebuild or a restart reloads then, and one with nothing new keeps its state. Measured after: 48 page changes with one or two connections open, 24 with prerendering at most four. The stream sends `: ping` every 5s, since Bun closes a connection idle for 10s.
- `site:dev` and `site:build` both write `site/public` and `site/assets.json`, and the dev server restarts when `assets.json` changes and rebuilds in dev mode — stop it before a build.
- The function's `.vc-config.json` (`output()`) copies what Vercel's own Elysia preset writes for a Bun function — `runtime: bun1.x`, `launcherType: Nodejs` — since the Build Output API documents no Bun config. Before changing a field, compare it with what the preset writes now: `vercel build` on a copy without `framework: null` (the project's dashboard preset is Elysia) whose entry, named by `outputDirectory`, imports `elysia`. Leave `architecture` out: a local build fills in the machine's own.
